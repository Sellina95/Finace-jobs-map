# Finance Jobs Map 🌍

**Finance Jobs Map** is an interactive visual map designed to show where financial roles sit within the broader financial system — and how institutions, markets, infrastructure, functions, and jobs connect with one another.

🌐 **[Explore the Live Finance Jobs Map](https://sellina95.github.io/Finace-jobs-map/)**

## Why I Built This

While working in finance, I found that it was relatively easy to understand what my own team did, but much harder to see the bigger picture.

What do other teams actually do?

Where does each function sit within the financial system?

Which markets and products does a role interact with?

How does one team connect with Sales, Trading, Risk, Operations, Research, Treasury, or other parts of an institution?

And how do all of these functions ultimately connect to the markets and infrastructure around them?

These questions became even more relevant while I was exploring different career opportunities. Finance contains a huge number of roles, but job titles and job descriptions often explain an individual position without showing where that position fits into the wider system.

Finance Jobs Map started as an attempt to make that bigger picture easier to see.

## Who Is It For?

The project is designed to make the structure of the financial industry easier to explore regardless of someone's starting level of financial knowledge.

It may be useful for:

- **Finance professionals** who want to understand functions outside their own team and how different parts of the industry interact.
- **Students and job seekers** trying to understand what different financial roles actually do and where they sit within the industry.
- **Career changers** exploring unfamiliar areas of finance.
- **People outside finance** who simply want a visual way to understand how the financial system and its jobs are organized.

Rather than presenting finance as a collection of isolated job descriptions, the goal is to provide a navigable map:

**Financial System → Institution → Function → Role → Market → Product → Work → Relationships → Infrastructure**

## Design Principle

Finance Jobs Map is intended as an educational navigation tool, not a universal organizational chart.

Job titles, reporting lines, desk structures, and responsibilities vary across institutions, countries, business models, and market structures.

The taxonomy therefore focuses on broadly recognizable industry functions and relationships while allowing the map to evolve as additional roles and institutional structures are researched.

## Methodology & Scope

Finance Jobs Map is built from publicly available information about financial institutions, markets, market infrastructure, and professional roles, including official institutional materials, regulatory and market-structure documentation, and publicly available role descriptions.

The map does not reproduce the organizational structure of any single institution. Instead, it aims to identify broadly recognizable functions, role families, products, and relationships that can provide a useful common framework for navigating the financial industry.

Financial organizations are not structured identically. Team names, job titles, responsibilities, reporting lines, product coverage, and the placement of individual functions can vary significantly across institutions, jurisdictions, and business models. 

A function shown under one category in this map may therefore sit elsewhere — or be divided across several teams — at a particular institution.

The map should be understood as an educational and navigational framework rather than a universal or authoritative industry taxonomy.

As the project develops, the taxonomy and role descriptions will continue to be refined using additional primary and institutional sources, practitioner feedback, and cross-market comparisons.

## Long-Term Vision

Finance is the starting point.

Once the financial-industry map becomes sufficiently developed, the longer-term idea is to explore whether the same concept can be expanded into other industries — such as technology, healthcare, energy, aerospace, and beyond.

Each industry would require its own native structure rather than being forced into the financial-system framework.

The broader ambition is simple:

**make the world of work easier to navigate by showing not only what jobs do, but where they exist, what they interact with, and how they connect to the larger system around them.**

In other words: one map for finance first — and, eventually, perhaps a much bigger map of how jobs across industries fit together. 🌍
